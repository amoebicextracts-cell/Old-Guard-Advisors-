What does a cannabis operations consultant do?

A cannabis operations consultant diagnoses operational risk and helps turn recommendations into workable systems. The scope may include cultivation and extraction workflows, facility design and commissioning, equipment selection, SOPs, GMP and quality systems, staffing plans, product commercialization, and the records needed to support safe, repeatable production.

When should a cannabis operator bring in an advisor?

The highest-leverage time is before a costly decision becomes difficult to reverse: before signing off on a facility layout, purchasing major equipment, promising a launch date, entering a new product category, or responding to an audit finding.

An advisor can also help when yield, quality, downtime, documentation, or accountability problems continue without a clear root cause.

What should a cannabis GMP readiness assessment include?

A useful GMP readiness assessment follows actual material and records from intake through finished product. It examines batch records, deviations, corrective actions, training, sanitation, document control, traceability, quality ownership, and the gap between written procedures and floor practice.

Findings should be prioritized by risk, business impact, and the team's ability to sustain the fix.

Why do cannabis facility launches get delayed?

Most delays come from sequencing, not construction alone. Licensing, SOP development, hiring and training, long-lead equipment, utility commissioning, inspections, and the physical build must run as one coordinated schedule. The opening date is controlled by whichever track finishes last.

How should an operator evaluate a cannabis extraction system?

Evaluate reliable production rather than advertised throughput alone. Compare verified uptime, parts availability, service response, solvent recovery, utility and permitting requirements, operator training, safety, cleanability, and five-year operating cost against the actual material, building, and staffing conditions in the facility.

Can Old Guard work remotely or outside New York?

Yes. Old Guard supports remote and on-site engagements worldwide. The right delivery model depends on the problem: document and data reviews can often begin remotely, while facility flow, commissioning, training, and equipment issues may require time on site.

What happens during the first Old Guard conversation?

The first conversation defines the operating problem, current constraints, decisions already made, desired outcome, and urgency. If Old Guard is a fit, the next step is a focused assessment with a clear scope, evidence request, deliverables, and decision points—not an open-ended consulting engagement.
